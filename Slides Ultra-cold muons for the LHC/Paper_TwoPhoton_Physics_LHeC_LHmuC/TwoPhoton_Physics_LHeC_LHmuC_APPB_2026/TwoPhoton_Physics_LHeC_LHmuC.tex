\begin{abstract}
\noindent
High-energy photon-photon scattering is a complementary component of the 
physics programme at future CERN lepton-proton facilities. We examine the equivalent-photon description 
of elastic and proton-dissociative two-photon reactions and discuss their prospects at 
the Large Hadron-electron Collider (LHeC) and a proposed muon-proton collider (LH$\mu$C). 
The photon luminosity determines the rate of a hard two-photon subprocess. 
Two illustrative precision benchmarks are emphasized: tau-lepton pair production as a probe of 
electromagnetic dipole interactions, and $W$-boson pair production as a test of the 
electroweak quartic gauge sector. We distinguish previously studied collider benchmarks from newer, 
higher-energy muon-beam proposals and discuss how high-invariant-mass 
distributions may amplify beyond-Standard-Model effects. Loop-induced neutral gauge-boson production, 
top pairs, charged electroweak states and light-by-light scattering provide 
additional opportunities. The quantitative interpretation of anomalous-coupling effects requires 
consistent photon-emission selections, effective-field-theory validity checks and detector-level studies.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%===========================================================

Photon-photon fusion offers a particularly transparent route to the production of 
electrically charged and electroweakly coupled final states. A relativistic lepton and proton radiate photons, 
which subsequently undergo a hard collision, $\gamma\gamma\to X$. The radiating particles may 
remain intact, giving a fully exclusive topology, or the proton may dissociate into a low- or high-mass hadronic system. 
This separation of electromagnetic emission and the short-distance subprocess underlies the 
equivalent photon approximation (EPA)~\cite{Budnev1975,Drees1989}.

The proposed Large Hadron-electron Collider (LHeC) combines a lepton beam of order $50\GeV$ with 
the $7\TeV$ LHC proton beam, yielding $\sqrt{s_{ep}}\simeq1.2\TeV$~\cite{LHeCCDR2012,LHeC2025}. 
An electron-proton environment is especially attractive for exclusive studies because it 
has one rather than two colored incoming beams and is expected to have much less event 
pileup than high-luminosity proton-proton collisions. Neither advantage removes 
detector inefficiencies, proton dissociation or all soft-interaction uncertainties, but both simplify 
the experimental interpretation of photon-fusion signatures~\cite{ForthommeDIS2024,ForthommeICHEP2024}.

A complementary LH$\mu$C concept uses energetic positive muons against LHC proton beams. 
%Early two-photon studies employed $E_{\mu}=0.5\TeV$, corresponding to $\sqrt{s_{\mu p}}\simeq3.74\TeV$. 
The recent proposal of Ref.~\cite{Piotrzkowski2026} emphasizes $1$ and $2\TeV$ muon beams, leading to $\sqrt{s_{\mu p}}\simeq5.3$ and $7.5\TeV$, 
respectively, with a projected average luminosity approaching $10^{34}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-2}\mathrm{s}^{-1}$. 
These are machine scenarios: published photon-fusion predictions must be associated with the 
particular energy and photon selections actually used in each calculation.

We outline the EPA and the energy dependence of its luminosity function, and focus on two physics 
cases that connect Standard-Model (SM) cross sections with higher-dimensional 
interactions: $\gamma\gamma\to\tau^+\tau^-$ and $\gamma\gamma\to W^+W^-$. Other charged and neutral final 
states complete a broader programme in which a high-energy photon spectrum, clean event 
topology and differential observables are complementary assets.

\section{Theoretical framework for two-photon interactions}

\subsection{Equivalent photon approximation}
%===========================================================


For $\ell=e^-$ or $\mu^+$, the generic reaction is $\ell p\to\ell+X+p^{(*)}$. 
In the quasi-real limit the cross section can be written as a convolution of photon 
spectra and the elementary photon-photon cross section,

%--------------------------------------
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\ell p\to\ell Xp^{(*)}} = \int_0^1dy_\ell\int_0^1dy_p\,\Phi_\ell(y_\ell)\Phi_p(y_p)\,\hat\sigma_{\gamma\gamma\to X}(\wgg),
\label{eq:epa}
\end{equation}
%--------------------------------------

where $y_i$ is the fractional energy carried by the photon and $\Phi_i(y_i)=dN_{\gamma/i}/dy_i$. 
Here $\wgg$ denotes the diphoton invariant mass, with $\wgg^2\simeq y_\ell y_p s_{\ell p}$ for 
collinear, quasi-real emission. Its natural generalization is

%--------------------------------------
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\sigma_{\ell p}&=\int_{W_{\rm thr}}^{\sqrt{s_{\ell p}}}dW\,\sgg(W)\,\hat\sigma_{\gamma\gamma\to X}(W),\\
\sgg(W)&=\frac{2W}{s_{\ell p}}\int_{W^2/s_{\ell p}}^1\frac{dy_\ell}{y_\ell}\,\Phi_\ell(y_\ell)\Phi_p\!\left(\frac{W^2}{s_{\ell p}y_\ell}\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:lum}
\end{equation}
%--------------------------------------

Thus $\sgg$ has dimensions of inverse energy, and its integral over $W$ gives a 
dimensionless effective two-photon luminosity fraction. Equation~(\ref{eq:lum}) is a 
collinear EPA identity, not an exact statement at arbitrarily high photon virtuality.

\subsection{Equivalent-photon spectra and photon virtuality}
%===========================================================

For a point-like lepton of mass $m_\ell$, an illustrative virtuality-integrated EPA spectrum is~\cite{Budnev1975}

%--------------------------------------
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_\ell(y)&=\frac{\alpha}{2\pi}\left[\frac{1+(1-y)^2}{y}\ln\frac{Q_{\max}^2}{Q_{\min}^2}-\frac{2(1-y)}{y}\left(1-\frac{Q_{\min}^2}{Q_{\max}^2}\right)\right],\\
Q_{\min}^2&\simeq\frac{m_\ell^2y^2}{1-y}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:lepton}
\end{equation}
%--------------------------------------

for $Q_{\min}^2<Q_{\max}^2$, with a vanishing flux otherwise. The muon mass 
increases $Q_{\min}^2$ relative to an electron at fixed $y$: the gain from a 
muon collider therefore comes from its accessible beam energy and usable phase space, 
not from a universally larger flux at fixed energy.
For an elastic proton, an often used representation is

%--------------------------------------
\begin{equation}
\Phi_p^{\eel}(y)=\frac{\alpha}{\pi y}\int_{Q_{\min}^2}^{Q_{\max}^2}\frac{dQ^2}{Q^2}\left[(1-y)\left(1-\frac{Q_{\min}^2}{Q^2}\right)F_E(Q^2)+\frac{y^2}{2}F_M(Q^2)\right],
\label{eq:proton}
\end{equation}
%--------------------------------------

where $F_E=(4m_p^2G_E^2+Q^2G_M^2)/(4m_p^2+Q^2)$ and $F_M=G_M^2$ involve the Sachs 
electromagnetic form factors~\cite{Drees1989}. Here $Q^2=-q^2>0$ denotes the photon 
virtuality, and the integration bounds must be matched to the tagging or acceptance cuts. 
For the proton-dissociative channel $p\to\gamma^*+N^*$, the photon spectrum can instead 
be expressed through measured inclusive electromagnetic structure 
functions $F_2(x,Q^2)$ and $F_L(x,Q^2)$~\cite{Manohar2016,Manohar2017}. With $M_N$ the 
dissociation mass, $x=Q_p^2/(M_N^2-m_p^2+Q_p^2)$, so that

%--------------------------------------
\begin{equation}
M_N^2-m_p^2=Q_p^2\left(\frac{1}{x}-1\right).
\label{eq:mn}
\end{equation}
%--------------------------------------

An expanded dissociation phase space can raise the predicted rate, but it changes 
the final-state topology and increases sensitivity to the structure-function treatment. 
In particular, an inclusive photon PDF and a proton-tagged elastic flux should not be 
regarded as interchangeable inputs.



%===========================================================
\subsection{Validity of the approximation}
%===========================================================

For $Q_\ell^2,Q_p^2\ll W_{\gamma\gamma}^2$, transverse quasi-real
photons dominate, and Eqs.~(\ref{eq:epa})-(\ref{eq:lum}) provide
a well-motivated factorized description of photon-induced
lepton-proton interactions. The commonly used high-energy relation
$W_{\gamma\gamma}^2\simeq y_\ell y_p s_{\ell p}$, however, neglects
kinematic corrections associated with photon virtualities and
proton dissociation. Within the kinematic conventions adopted in
the present EPA treatment, the corrected expressions for the
two-photon invariant mass are

%--------------------------------------
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
W_{\gamma\gamma,\mathrm{el}}^2
&= -Q_\ell^2+y_\ell y_p s_{\ell p},
\\[3pt]
W_{\gamma\gamma,\mathrm{inel}}^2
&= -Q_\ell^2-Q_p^2+y_\ell y_p s_{\ell p}
\\
&\quad
+\frac{Q_\ell^2}{s_{\ell p}}
\left(Q_p^2+M_N^2-m_p^2\right).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:W_corrected}
\end{equation}
%--------------------------------------


Here $Q_\ell^2$ and $Q_p^2$ denote the virtualities of the photons
emitted by the lepton and proton, respectively, $y_\ell$ and $y_p$
are their longitudinal energy fractions, and $s_{\ell p}$ is the
squared lepton-proton centre-of-mass energy. The mass $M_N$
characterizes the proton-dissociative hadronic system, while
$m_p$ denotes the proton mass. The elastic expression explicitly
retains the lepton-side virtuality correction, whereas the
proton-dissociative expression also depends on $Q_p^2$ and $M_N$.

Larger photon virtualities can modify the $\gamma^*\gamma^*$
matrix elements, accessible phase space, and contributions from
longitudinal photon polarizations. Consequently, full
virtuality-dependent calculations become preferable when
$Q_{\ell,p}^2$ is not negligible compared with
$W_{\gamma\gamma}^2$. Comparisons with matrix-element generators
can yield percent-level agreement for elastic production when
consistent kinematic definitions and phase-space selections are
employed, while proton-dissociative predictions are more sensitive
to the allowed $M_N$ and $Q_p^2$ ranges. Accurate reconstruction
of $W_{\gamma\gamma}$ and explicit control of the photon-virtuality
assumptions are therefore essential for reliable comparisons
between the LHeC and LH$\mu$C configurations.

\section{Tau-pair production and electromagnetic moments}

\subsection{Standard-Model production}
%===========================================================


The tree-level process $\gamma\gamma\to\tau^+\tau^-$ is governed by 
QED $t$- and $u$-channel tau exchange. The relatively low production 
threshold makes this channel valuable for photon-luminosity calibration, 
while the large accessible invariant mass enables a test of the $\gamma\tau\tau$ 
vertex well above the tau mass. The first observation of photon-induced tau-pair 
production in $pp$ collisions, together with bounds on tau electromagnetic moments, 
has already demonstrated the utility of this mechanism~\cite{CMS2024}.

\subsection{Dipole interactions and their EFT interpretation}
%===========================================================


A convenient on-shell description of non-standard tau electromagnetic interactions uses

%--------------------------------------
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\tau\tau\gamma}^{\rm dip}=\frac{e\,\delta a_\tau}{4m_\tau}\,\bar\tau\sigma^{\mu\nu}\tau F_{\mu\nu}-\frac{i\,d_\tau}{2}\,\bar\tau\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_5\tau F_{\mu\nu},
\label{eq:dipole}
\end{equation}
%--------------------------------------


where $\delta a_\tau$ denotes the deviation from the SM magnetic moment and $d_\tau$ 
is the electric dipole moment; the sign conventions are conventional and must be kept 
fixed when matching amplitudes. The magnetic term is CP even, whereas the electric term 
violates CP. At scales above electroweak symmetry breaking, the corresponding new-physics 
interactions may be embedded in gauge-invariant dimension-six SMEFT operators,


%--------------------------------------
\begin{equation}
\mathcal O_{eB}^{33}=(\bar L_3\sigma^{\mu\nu}\tau_R)H B_{\mu\nu},\qquad \mathcal O_{eW}^{33}=(\bar L_3\sigma^{\mu\nu}\tau_R)\sigma^I H W^I_{\mu\nu},
\label{eq:tauops}
\end{equation}
%--------------------------------------


with $\Delta\mathcal L=(C_{eB}^{33}\mathcal O_{eB}^{33}+C_{eW}^{33}\mathcal O_{eW}^{33})/\Lambda^2+\mathrm{h.c.}$. 
The physical photon coupling depends on the weak mixing angle and on the real and imaginary parts 
of the Wilson-coefficient combination. The exact numerical matching should follow the 
normalization chosen for the SU(2) generators and the dipole vertex.




%===========================================================
\subsection{High-mass distributions and sensitivity}
%===========================================================

The amplitude may be organized schematically as $\mathcal M=\mathcal M_{\rm SM}+\delta a_\tau\mathcal M_a+d_\tau\mathcal M_d$. 
Consequently a differential distribution contains interference and higher-order dipole terms, 
while the CP-odd interference may vanish in sufficiently inclusive CP-even observables. 
The behavior of $d\sigma/dM_{\tau\tau}$, rather than only the integrated cross section, 
can therefore improve the separation of anomalous scenarios from the SM. This enhancement 
must be weighed against the rapid decrease in photon luminosity and the efficiency of 
reconstructing visible tau decays with escaping neutrinos.

Earlier exploratory plots used $\delta a_\tau=0.008$, whereas an updated study 
considered $\delta a_\tau=5\times10^{-4}$. Because the original numerical files 
and curve definitions are not available in the present portable package, we do not 
reproduce or relabel the corresponding distributions. A genuine exclusion analysis 
would need binned event yields, backgrounds, systematic uncertainties, correlations 
and an explicit treatment of the dipole operator's range of validity. It is therefore 
more accurate here to identify the high-$M_{\tau\tau}$ region as a 
promising \emph{observable} than to advertise a new confidence-level reach.

\section{$W$-boson pairs and anomalous quartic gauge couplings}
\subsection{Standard-Model $\gamma\gamma\to W^+W^-$}
%===========================================================


At leading order $\gamma\gamma\to W^+W^-$ proceeds through charged-$W$ exchange 
in the $t$ and $u$ channels, together with the four-point $\gamma\gamma W^+W^-$ vertex. 
Gauge invariance ties these amplitudes together. Unlike massive charged 
scalar or fermion-pair production, the unpolarized high-energy vector-pair 
subprocess retains a sizable rate, making it an unusually productive probe of the 
electroweak gauge sector~\cite{ForthommeICHEP2024}. Its threshold is $W_{\rm thr}=2m_W$, 
substantially higher than for tau pairs.


For the elastic benchmarks summarized in Table~\ref{tab:rates},
the $W^+W^-$ production cross section at the LH$\mu$C
exceeds the corresponding LHeC reference value by factors
of approximately $4.5$ and $7.0$ at $\sqrt{s}=3.7$ and
$5.3\TeV$, respectively. Assuming an illustrative integrated
luminosity of $250\fb^{-1}$ for each configuration, the
corresponding numbers of produced $W$-boson pairs are
$9.25\times10^3$ at the LHeC, compared with
$4.15\times10^4$ and $6.45\times10^4$ at the LH$\mu$C
for $\sqrt{s}=3.7$ and $5.3\TeV$, respectively,
before accounting for branching fractions, detector
acceptance, and reconstruction efficiencies.

These results illustrate the substantial enhancement of
photon-induced electroweak production at the LH$\mu$C,
driven by its higher collision energy and harder
photon-photon luminosity spectrum. The increased event
rates, together with the extended high-invariant-mass
reach, provide promising opportunities for more precise
measurements and improved sensitivity to anomalous
electroweak interactions. Quantitative sensitivity
projections, however, require consistent event selections,
background estimates, and detector-level reconstruction.




%===========================================================
\subsection{Dimension-eight quartic interactions}
%===========================================================


Anomalous quartic gauge couplings (aQGCs) are conveniently parameterized in a 
linearly realized electroweak EFT using higher-dimensional operators. 
A generic representation is

%--------------------------------------
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\rm EFT}=\mathcal L_{\rm SM}+\sum_i\frac{f_i}{\Lambda^4}\mathcal O_i^{(8)}+\cdots,\qquad \sigma=\sigma_{\rm SM}+\sum_i\frac{f_i}{\Lambda^4}\sigma_{{\rm int},i}+\sum_{ij}\frac{f_if_j}{\Lambda^8}\sigma_{{\rm quad},ij}.
\label{eq:aqgc}
\end{equation}
%--------------------------------------


The commonly used $S$, $M$ and $T$ operator families organize 
contributions involving covariant Higgs derivatives and electroweak 
field strengths~\cite{Eboli2006,Eboli2016}. For example, in one standard 
convention $\mathcal O_{M2}=(B_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu})[(D_\beta H)^\dagger D^\beta H]$. 
A benchmark such as $f_{M2}/\Lambda^4=1\TeV^{-4}$ changes the amplitude, 
but is \emph{not} by itself an experimental constraint. Equation~(\ref{eq:aqgc}) 
also makes clear that large positive high-mass deviations can be dominated by the 
quadratic EFT term, potentially in a regime where the truncation becomes unreliable.




%===========================================================
\subsection{Comparison and EFT domain of validity}
%===========================================================


The principal sensitivity of $\gamma\gamma\to WW$ to anomalous contributions 
occurs in high-$M_{WW}$ bins, where increased hard-scattering energy may offset 
declining photon luminosity. A collider-to-collider comparison must hold the EFT 
coefficients, final-state acceptance, photon virtuality cuts and proton-emission 
topology fixed. In particular, the original LH$\mu$C aQGC distribution used a $5.3\TeV$ 
collider configuration, while the elastic SM cross-section number just quoted 
corresponds to $3.7\TeV$; one must not mix them into a single numerical limit. 
Furthermore, an LHC proton--proton comparison is affected by proton survival 
probabilities, pileup and tagging conditions that have no simple counterpart in $\ell p$ scattering.

An experimentally interpretable analysis would report the selected reconstructed 
final state, detector response, background template, correlated uncertainties and 
treatment of parameter degeneracies. It would also specify an energy domain such that 
the operator expansion remains controlled. 
A visible ratio $[d\sigma/dM_{WW}]_{\rm EFT}/[d\sigma/dM_{WW}]_{\rm SM}$ signals a 
potentially discriminating observable; it is not equivalent to a detector-level 95\% 
confidence interval. This distinction is central when comparing upgraded LH$\mu$C energies 
with earlier baseline studies.

\section{Additional Standard-Model and BSM opportunities}
%===========================================================

Beyond tau and $W$ pairs, several photon-fusion processes become
particularly interesting as the accessible diphoton invariant mass
increases. The neutral final state $\gamma\gamma\to ZZ$ is
loop-induced in the SM, making its high-mass spectrum a promising
probe of anomalous neutral quartic gauge interactions.
The process $\gamma\gamma\to t\bar t$ provides a complementary
electromagnetic production mechanism for the heaviest SM fermion
and offers sensitivity to possible deviations in its photon
couplings. With a production threshold near $2m_t$, its rate
depends strongly on the high-energy tail of $\sgg$ and must
be distinguished from the substantially larger
$\gamma g$ top-quark photoproduction channel.

Charged electroweak states provide further opportunities for
probing physics beyond the SM. Higgsino-like chargino and
charged-slepton pairs can be produced through
$\gamma\gamma\to\widetilde H^+\widetilde H^-$ and
$\gamma\gamma\to\widetilde\ell^+\widetilde\ell^-$,
respectively. Their leading electromagnetic interactions
are determined by the electric charge, while their spin and
mass govern the threshold behaviour and angular distributions.
Compressed mass spectra remain experimentally challenging
because the visible decay products may be soft and the
missing-momentum signatures require accurate event
reconstruction. Existing LHC searches already constrain
parts of this parameter space, making photon-induced
production at lepton--hadron colliders a complementary
approach.

Finally, the loop-induced processes
$\gamma\gamma\to\gamma\gamma$ and $\gamma\gamma\to H$
provide additional probes of charged virtual particles
and potential BSM contributions. Their relatively small
production rates emphasize the importance of controlling
photon spectra, exclusive-event selections, and backgrounds.
Compared with the LHeC, the higher centre-of-mass energies
available at the LH$\mu$C lead to a harder photon--photon
luminosity spectrum, enhancing the accessible high-$W$
region. This advantage is particularly relevant for heavy
final states, where production thresholds strongly suppress
the LHeC rates, and for anomalous interactions whose effects
grow with energy. Consequently, the LH$\mu$C offers the
potential for substantially improved mass reach and
enhanced sensitivity to electroweak and BSM interactions,
subject to realistic luminosity, acceptance, and background
assumptions. These additional channels significantly broaden
the photon-induced physics programme while complementing
the precision studies of tau and $W$-boson pairs.

\section{Experimental considerations and future prospects}
%===========================================================



A reliable photon-fusion analysis must simultaneously account for
the outgoing-lepton acceptance, central-system reconstruction, and
proton final state. Elastic channels may exploit forward proton
tagging, large rapidity gaps, small transverse-momentum imbalance,
and acoplanarity, whereas proton-dissociative channels require
either explicit hadronic reconstruction or a carefully modelled
inclusive treatment. Tau decays introduce neutrinos, while
$W$-boson reconstruction involves branching fractions, combinatorial
ambiguities, and missing-energy effects. Searches for new charged
particles may additionally be limited by detector acceptance and
reconstruction efficiencies. These experimental effects are not
fully captured by generator-level EPA calculations.

The machine-detector interface for the LH$\mu$C remains under
development~\cite{Piotrzkowski2026}. Quantitative sensitivity
studies must therefore account for muon-beam-induced backgrounds,
beam optics, forward-tagging acceptance, and realistic integrated
luminosities. Future investigations should combine validated
photon fluxes with full matrix-element calculations where
appropriate, detector simulations, and statistical analyses
consistent with EFT validity requirements. The extended calculations
at $\sqrt{s}=3.7$, $5.3$, and $7.5\TeV$ enable a systematic
comparison of the LH$\mu$C physics potential across different
beam-energy configurations. In particular, the enhanced
high-invariant-mass photon-photon luminosity provides greater
kinematic reach for heavy charged states and offers promising
opportunities for improved sensitivity to anomalous electroweak
interactions relative to the LHeC.




%===========================================================
\section{Summary and conclusions}
%===========================================================



High-energy photon fusion offers a complementary electroweak and BSM
physics programme at future CERN lepton-proton colliders. The EPA
organizes the connection between beam-dependent photon fluxes and
the hard $\gamma\gamma$ subprocess, while elastic and proton-dissociative
emission must remain distinct. Compared with the LHeC, the higher
beam energies attainable at the LH$\mu$C provide a harder
$\gamma\gamma$ luminosity spectrum and substantially enhance the
accessible high-invariant-mass region, offering improved sensitivity
reach to anomalous electroweak interactions and heavy charged states.
In particular, tau-pair spectra and $W$-pair production furnish
promising probes of anomalous electromagnetic dipole moments and
quartic gauge couplings, respectively, with potentially enhanced
sensitivity at the LH$\mu$C. Consistent kinematic selections,
reliable photon-emission models, detector response, and EFT validity
checks remain indispensable for establishing quantitative
sensitivity projections.
